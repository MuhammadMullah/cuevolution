Each circuit is a self-contained competition. Round 1 is a double-elimination phase that halves the field: a player must lose twice to go out. Everyone who survives Round 1 enters Round 2 with a clean sheet, and from there a single loss ends the run — straight knockout down to a champion.

Round 1 — double elimination, race to 6

Round 1 runs as four waves of matches. The winners’ side and the losers’ side both play, and the two sides meet once, in the crossover, before the round closes.

The ladies’ field: 55 players in a 64 bracket

55 entrants are drawn into a 64-slot bracket, leaving 9 byes. Byes are assigned by random draw among all 55, with one structural constraint described below.

Draw constraint — two byes must never feed the same losers’ match Each losers’-bracket R1 match is fed by the losers of two specific winners’-bracket R1 matches. A bye produces no loser, so it leaves that feeder slot empty. If both feeders of one losers’ match are byes, that match has no players at all and the losers’ side delivers only 15 players into the crossover — which needs exactly 16. The draw must therefore place at most one bye per losers’-bracket pairing. With 9 byes across 16 pairings this is always satisfiable, but it has to be enforced when the draw is made, not checked afterwards.
Why 9 and not some other number The bye count is whatever brings the field up to the next power of two: 64 − 55 = 9. If entries change at Circuits 2 and 3, the bye count changes with them. Below 33 entrants the bracket should drop to 32 slots rather than carry 30-plus byes.

Round 2 — straight knockout to a champion, race to 5

Round 2 opens with the 32 undefeated players meeting the 32 one-loss survivors. From that point the competition is a conventional single-elimination ladder: lose once and you are out, regardless of how you arrived.

Records from Round 1 do not carry forward — a player who arrives with one loss is on equal footing with an undefeated player. The only advantage of arriving undefeated is in the pairing: the opening Round 2 match is always undefeated versus one-loss survivor, never two undefeated players against each other.

Sequencing constraint The waves are strictly ordered: LB R1 cannot start until every WB R1 match is finished, and the crossover needs both WB R2 and LB R1 complete. Those two synchronisation points are where a single slow match holds up the whole room, so they are worth scheduling around.

Confirmed for this draft

  • Round 2 runs to a champion in each category — the circuit is self-contained.
  • Round 2 is straight knockout, not a continuation of double elimination.
  • Ladies’ byes are drawn at random, not seeded by ranking or CuevoPoints.
  • The same format applies to all three circuits; field sizes and bye counts are recalculated per circuit from actual entries.

Open points for the committee

  1. Race lengths get shorter as the stakes rise. Round 1 is a race to 6 and Round 2 — including quarter-finals, semi-finals and the final — is a race to 5. This is the opposite of the usual pattern. Confirm it is deliberate, or set a longer race for the closing stages.
  2. Third-place playoff. The two beaten semi-finalists currently finish joint third. Is a playoff wanted, for placings or for prize money?
  3. Round 1 draw rules. Is the opening draw purely random, or are there restrictions such as keeping players from the same venue or region apart in early matches?
  4. Round 2 re-draw. Is the undefeated-versus-survivor pairing drawn fresh at the start of Round 2, or fixed by bracket position carried over from Round 1?
  5. How the three circuits combine. Each circuit produces a champion. What carries forward to the Finals — circuit champions only, accumulated CuevoPoints, or a ranking across all three?
  6. No-shows and walkovers. Does a no-show count as one loss (dropping the player to the losers’ side) or as immediate elimination?
  7. Ladies’ entries below 33 or above 64 at Circuits 2 and 3 — agree the bracket-size rule now rather than at the draw.

Appendix — what this needs from the platform

For the engineering team. The Circuit stage already exists in the system as a capped, knockout-bracket stage, but this format needs more than the current model expresses.

AreaTodayNeeded
Bracket model knockout_brackets — one row per stage and category, with rounds hanging off it. No concept of a winners’ or losers’ side. A way to express two sides and the crossover between them.
Loser routing fixtures record two participants and a result. Where the loser goes next is not modelled — single elimination never needed it. Each fixture needs a destination for the loser as well as the winner.
Race length No race_to field exists anywhere in the schema. Race length per round — 6 in Round 1, 5 in Round 2 — so results can be validated against it.
Round ordering rounds carry a name only. Explicit ordering, so the four Round 1 waves and six Round 2 rounds sequence deterministically.
Byes fixtures.walkover_kind exists for walkovers. A bye is not a walkover — nobody forfeits. Needs its own representation, plus the draw constraint on page 3.
Draw scope draws requires exactly one of venue_id or region_id, and formula_group_count is mandatory. A Circuit draw is national and has no groups, so it fits neither slot as the table stands.
